# minimal — marketing starter

This folder contains the product experience. Use this small operating plan to
turn it into repeatable, bilingual promotion without giving away the original
wallpapers.

## First seven posts

1. **What minimal is** — Japanese and English in one post. Show a watermarked
   sample in a phone mockup and link to the product collection.
2. **The problem** — a noisy home screen / a crowded week, then show the calm
   alternative. Use one short idea, not a list of features.
3. **Still Water is free** — lead to the free product as the lowest-friction
   first purchase and library test.
4. **Choose one** — carousel or short video of three sample forms (Dune, Tide,
   Fog); overlay `SAMPLE · minimal.` on every exported visual.
5. **One week, less noise** — show the Schedule template as the practical
   counterpart to the wallpaper.
6. **How it arrives** — sign in → purchase → library → download, in a short
   screen recording. This reduces purchase anxiety.
7. **Ask a single question** — `Which helps you reset: a clear plan or a quiet
   screen? / 予定を整える？画面を整える？` and reply to genuine comments.

## Posting guardrails

- Keep each Japanese and English version in the same post; do not use browser
  auto-translation for product claims.
- Use preview/sample images only. Never publish the source wallpaper file.
- Put a visible, non-removable `SAMPLE · minimal.` watermark across every
  social export, while keeping the underlying artwork recognisable.
- Start with 1–2 posts per day across all platforms. Increase only when
  saves, profile visits, and product-page clicks remain healthy.
- Every post has one destination: free wallpaper, wallpaper collection, or
  minimal Schedule. Never ask for multiple actions at once.

## What to measure weekly

- Product-page visits from each post.
- Checkout starts and completed purchases.
- Free-download-to-paid-product conversion.
- Saves, shares, and profile visits (not only views).
- Answers to the optional Accounts survey.

## Automation boundary

Metricool can schedule prepared assets and copy. Posts, replies, and direct
messages represent the brand publicly, so review the first calendar before it
is published. Keep the source artwork in the private delivery bucket only.
